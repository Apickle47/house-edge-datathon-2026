# Luck, Location, or a Leak? The Gilded Ace bottom ten

**The question.** Management wants to replace the ten machines that earned the least from January to June 2025. We tested three explanations before spending.

**Luck.** For every machine we computed the house-win standard deviation from each game's published payout variance and the actual bets. Luck z = (house win minus theo) divided by that deviation. Across 310 machines the z-scores follow the curve pure chance predicts: 5 fall below −2.33, where chance expects about 3. Most of the bottom ten have ordinary luck.

**Location.** Nine of the ten sit in the Back Forty. Identical game math earns 35–50% less theo per available hour there than on the main floor. A natural experiment confirms the cause: while the walkway from the Main Entrance was closed (Mar 3 to Apr 7), Back Forty theo per hour fell 29% as other zones rose 3%, then recovered after it reopened. Five of the ten also run a 95% RTP game that earns little by design, and three were on the floor only part of the period.

**Leak.** One machine, M-1158, pays on 38.6% of spins against a published 31.5% (z ≈ 124). It sits $36k below theo. That is a reason to inspect the paytable, not proof of wrongdoing. Downtime is small: availability is 99.6%, though Ridgeline machines fault two to four times as often and overnight faults wait longest.

**Players and comps.** Tracked guests wagered $163.8M, received $148.2M and lost $15.6M, almost exactly theo; 16% of player-months ended ahead. The top 10% of players carry 64% of theo. In late April the points needed per comp dollar fell from about 12.5 to 5.8, and reinvestment rose from 1.19% to 1.31% of theo without more play.

**Model.** We trained on January to April and tested on May to June. A weekday-mean forecast of daily coin-in cut mean absolute error by 69% versus a flat average ($69k vs $218k per day). The result sets when to schedule disruptive work (Tuesday and Wednesday mornings) and argues against buying a promotion weekend.

**Recommendation ($17,500 of $100,000).** Inspect five machines led by M-1158 ($6,000). Refresh Back Forty wayfinding ($7,500), measured by difference-in-differences against other zones. Run a randomized comp pilot ($4,000) before any floor-wide change. Keep $82,500. Replacing the ten would cost $33,800 to $220,000 and fix none of the causes.

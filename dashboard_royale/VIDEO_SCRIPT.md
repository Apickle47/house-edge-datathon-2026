"Management wants to replace the ten machines that earned the least. We tested whether that's luck, location or a leak before spending a dollar."

Nine of ten sit in the Back Forty, five run a 95% payout game, and three weren't on the floor all year." Point at the scatter: "The bottom ten aren't bad machines. They're in a weak spot."

"One real lead: M-1158 pays on 38.6% of spins when its paytable says 31.5%. That's not luck. We'd inspect it, without assuming wrongdoing."

**1:20–1:35 · Model** (forecast chart)
"Trained on January to April, tested on May and June: forecasting by weekday cuts the error by 69% versus a flat average. We use it to schedule disruptive work on quiet Tuesday and Wednesday mornings and to skip a promotion weekend."

We'd keep $82,500. Replacing the ten would cost $34,000 to $220,000 and fix none of these causes."
